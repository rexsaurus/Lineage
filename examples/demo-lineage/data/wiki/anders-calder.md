**Anders Calder** (about 1883 – 1951) was a Norwegian emigrant who worked the iron-ore boats and docks at Duluth,
Minnesota, and the grandfather of [[Ruth Calder]].[^m][^s1]

## Emigration
The family remembered that Anders crossed "on a ship when he was sixteen", wearing one pair of boots the whole way.[^s1]
The passenger manifest of the S.S. Ottavia, which arrived at Quebec from Liverpool on 21 May 1900, lists "Calder,
Anders", aged 17, of Aalesund, bound for Duluth.[^m] The record and the family account differ by a year; both are kept.

## Duluth
He worked on the ore boats and then at the docks.[^s1b] The 1910 census shows him in Duluth with his wife,
[[Signe Calder]], and their son [[Henrik Calder]], born that year.[^c] In October 1938 the payroll of the
[[Duluth Ore Dock Company]] listed "A. Calder" as dock foreman at Dock No. 6, with his son as chute tender.[^p] He died in
1951, according to the family.[^tl]

## Possessions
His painted pine sea chest, with his initials on the lid, survives with a great-grandson.[^r6]
